Their exclusion from the main table is by predeclared role, not by observed outcome. No physical endpoint remained unresolved in the 72 completed comparisons.

The known predictive construction resolves the selected failures of the historical and property-aligned one-step mechanisms, including the bounded-observation and one-second measurement-age plus one-second input-delay cases. The simple tracker also resolves these selected trajectories without an invariant-set certificate. These observations constrain novelty: merely resolving this midpoint or adding a delay-aware safety margin is not a new algorithmic contribution.

The barrier construction passes its own analytical and infeasibility fixtures, but six main runs encounter later loss of its sufficient-condition feasibility. Its 48 external tracking decisions remain distinct from barrier-certified actions. The table must not be used as a claim of uninterrupted barrier protection or as a refutation of a theorem outside its persistence assumptions.

A separate test solves one initial predictive plan, forces failure of the next 299 optimization attempts and uses 159 stored-plan and 140 terminal-feedback decisions. Independent fixed-input interval propagation retains containment and hold. This validates the implemented backup path under the declared ideal model; it is not hardware validation or a machine-checked feedback theorem.

The data reconstruct 21,600 decisions, 86,472 saved states and 3,378 accepted nominal plans. The maximum observed predictive policy-call time in the main comparisons is approximately 84.8 ms; initialization, common observation/proposal work, external continuation and offline adjudication have separate scope. All parameters, raw records, error conditions and runtime values are retained. The comparison is development data and cannot later be relabelled held out.
